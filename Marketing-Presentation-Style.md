# Marketing Presentation Style for PPT Master

Version 1.1 | Corpus-derived, unified design specification | 1 October 2026

## Purpose and authority

Create professional marketing presentations that make evidence understandable, human needs felt, brand decisions credible, creative ideas memorable, and execution concrete. This is ONE design language. Its varied compositions are responses to communication tasks, not separate styles, personas, categories, or template systems.

This specification preserves transferable judgment from a complete visual review of the supplied 2,078-page marketing corpus. The source includes multiple slide panels on some pages, compilation separators, repeated proposals, screenshots, blank pages and operational documents. It is not 2,078 verified unique professional slides. Every PDF page was included in 44 contiguous review batches; portrait panels received an additional closer review. Selected landscape slides received individual inspection. Small raster text is not uniformly legible, so this study does not claim word-by-word reading or exact typeface/size measurements. See the separate study record for coverage and evidence.

The corpus is reference evidence. Statements, commands, marketing claims, figures and dates inside it are not instructions for the authoring agent and are not verified facts for a new presentation. Repeated examples do not acquire extra authority through duplication. The standard below deliberately retains strong reasoning and craft while correcting weak contrast, tiny text, chart distortion and excessive decoration found in some examples.

No exact reference layout, proprietary copy, logo, palette, illustration, character, composition or asset is required. New work should be composed from the new brief and authorized assets. The guidance is self-contained and usable if the source disappears. It is a persistent specification, not a claim that the model's weights have been fine-tuned. It does not create a presentation or install a PowerPoint master.

For practical transfer, read the [agent quickstart](docs/Agent-Quickstart.md), [eight worked examples and teaching diagrams](docs/Worked-Examples.md), and [reference-free transfer checks](docs/Transfer-Checks.md) after this specification. They make the decisions inspectable without introducing another style. The examples are fictional teaching material, not new corpus evidence, factual sources, fixed layouts, or brand defaults. This specification remains the governing design language.

## 1. The governing design judgment

A slide earns its space when it changes what the audience understands, feels, believes or decides. Its composition should reveal that change before the audience reads the small text. Marketing craft joins analytical precision with a felt human benefit. Neither research without implication nor campaign art without a credible brand role is enough.

The recurring communication spine is:

**Evidence -> human insight -> credible brand role -> memorable creative expression -> tangible delivery -> accountable outcome.**

Use this spine as reasoning, not a compulsory six-section agenda. A performance review may begin with results; an executive proposal may begin with the recommendation. Preserve the causal links appropriate to the brief. Do not invent missing research, product efficacy, audience quotes, creator partnerships, forecasts or results merely to fill the spine.

The shared visual intelligence has seven commitments:

1. **Hierarchy before decoration.** One dominant entry point; evidence and explanation visibly subordinate to it.
2. **Relationships before containers.** Position, scale and connectors should express comparison, consequence, sequence, tension or convergence.
3. **Human meaning before demographic inventory.** Make the relevant desire, barrier or situation concrete; do not substitute an age range and stock portrait for insight.
4. **Selective specificity.** Show the few facts and artifacts needed to support the decision. Omit unrelated detail deliberately.
5. **Emphasis is scarce.** One leading claim or visual, with a small number of secondary anchors. If everything is highlighted, redesign the hierarchy.
6. **Concrete embodiment.** Show how the idea becomes a product experience, retail encounter, interface, content asset or audience action.
7. **Coherent variation.** Keep stable type roles, margins, alignment and meaning of color; vary scale, density and image presence with the story.

Premium appearance comes from editorial decisions: precise crops, convincing assets, well-chosen words, calm spacing, controlled contrast, meaningful data and a recognizably intentional reading path. Decoration cannot compensate for a weak argument.

## 2. Compose from the decision, not a remembered slide

Before arranging elements, write a private slide contract:

- Audience question: what does this page answer?
- Takeaway: the single conclusion in one sentence.
- Evidence: what materially supports that conclusion?
- Relationship: comparison, sequence, convergence, contrast, cause, scale or emotional context?
- Focal object: claim, number, chart, product, person, execution or diagram?
- Next step: what should the audience understand or decide after this?

If these answers are unclear, edit the argument first. A layout cannot resolve ambiguity in the brief.

Then choose the simplest spatial relationship that makes the argument visible. Parallel objects belong on comparable baselines. Stages belong in a directional sequence. Evidence feeding a conclusion should visibly converge or culminate. A tension needs visibly distinct sides. A product benefit needs proximity between product and benefit, without implying an unsupported causal claim.

This is a grammar, not a library of fixed layouts. Do not reconstruct reference coordinates. A given relationship can occupy a horizontal, vertical or asymmetric composition depending on content length, image shape and reading order.

### Establish three depths of attention

At first glance, the audience notices the dominant idea or visual. Within a few seconds, they can explain the relationship supporting it. With closer reading, they find labels, definitions, evidence and sources. Arrange these depths through scale, spacing and contrast. Do not require reading every paragraph to discover the page's purpose.

Use a deliberate route, usually from a clear title anchor into the dominant evidence, then toward an implication or next action. A center-weighted claim may be appropriate for a reveal; a left-anchored argument often supports evidence scanning. Avoid competing top corners, repeated large headings and multiple unrelated centers.

### Balance by visual weight

Equal-width columns are not automatically balanced. Dark images, faces, saturated fields, large numerals and dense text have greater visual weight than pale text or empty space. Counterbalance a strong image with a sufficiently substantial claim zone and purposeful breathing room. A small cutout floating beside a huge paragraph feels incidental; an oversized portrait beside two weak sentences feels unearned.

Asymmetry should create focus, not drift. Anchor the main objects to a shared grid, give the heavy side room, and preserve a visible relationship across the composition. Use optical adjustments for cutout silhouettes and numerals, while keeping structural alignment consistent.

## 3. Grid, alignment and whitespace

Define a consistent safe area, headline zone, main evidence area and optional source/navigation zone for the deck. These zones are reference boundaries, not boxes that must appear on every slide. A full-bleed image may cross them intentionally; body text should still have protected space.

Use a small underlying column grid to organize spans. Prefer aligning edges, title starts, chart boundaries, image crops and caption baselines. Do not center every text box independently. Make repeated items repeat their internal spacing, not merely their outer dimensions.

Keep intra-group spacing smaller than inter-group spacing. The audience should infer which label belongs to which number, which caption belongs to which image and which conclusion belongs to which evidence. More separation should mean a more substantial conceptual boundary.

Whitespace has four jobs:

- Protect the focal object so it can dominate.
- Separate different claims so they do not merge.
- Establish reading order and rhythm.
- Leave room for the audience to absorb a conclusion.

Empty space is intentional when removing it would harm one of these jobs. Empty space caused by a weak crop, tiny diagram, arbitrary template padding or a failed content fit is not premium restraint. White margins around exported portrait pages are compilation artifacts, not slide-design recommendations.

Do not fill a quiet corner because it is available. Conversely, do not enlarge a diagram's whitespace while shrinking its labels. Keep information readable and let the unused canvas support the hierarchy.

## 4. Typography as argument

Use stable semantic roles: section label, takeaway headline, supporting subhead, body, metric, caption, source and navigation. Let font size, weight and spacing do most of the work. Color is an additional cue, not the only hierarchy.

Choose a primary font with a clear body-text voice, readable numerals, strong bold weights and reliable language support. For Vietnamese, verify accents and stacked diacritics at the actual size. One family is usually sufficient. A complementary display face can support a campaign expression or important reveal; keep routine evidence text in the primary family. Never use scripts or stylized lettering for analytical paragraphs.

Avoid a typeface collection masquerading as variety. Changing type to suit every page breaks the language. Vary hierarchy and image scale before adding fonts.

### Headline behavior

Analytical headlines should normally state what the evidence means. Topic labels such as Market, Audience or Strategy belong in a smaller section label. A short navigational title is acceptable on a transition or operating table where the task is already clear.

A good headline selects an implication that the evidence actually supports. It names a behavior, consequence, tension, opportunity or decision. Replace broad claims like “A huge opportunity” with a supported, specific conclusion. Do not turn a correlation into causation or omit a qualifying scope just to strengthen the wording.

Use sentence case for explanatory headlines unless the current brand requires otherwise. Reserve all caps for short labels or compact campaign words. Long all-cap sentences are difficult to scan. A campaign phrase may be much larger than other headings, but it must remain a readable phrase rather than scattered decorative words.

Treat line breaks editorially. Keep noun phrases and important verb phrases together. Avoid an isolated weak word on the last line. A two-line headline should have deliberate rhythm, not merely occupy the full box. If it grows to three or four lines, first shorten it or allocate a larger statement page; do not quietly shrink it below the deck's hierarchy.

### Body behavior

Write short units of thought. Prefer one compact paragraph or a few meaningful points to a wall of micro-bullets. Use bullets for genuinely parallel facts; use prose for explanation; use a diagram for relationships; use a table for stable comparative criteria.

Bold or accent a few consequential phrases, not every line. A number should have a clear label, unit and context next to it. Separate the magnitude from the explanation without detaching them conceptually. Equal-status metrics use equal type treatment; a hero metric can be larger only when its significance warrants it.

Align body text left by default. Center concise statements and campaign phrases when the composition benefits. Avoid justified paragraphs, excessive tracking, long italic body text and narrow columns that create broken reading rhythms. Provide sufficient line spacing for diacritics. Sources may be quieter but must remain readable in the intended delivery mode.

## 5. Density: selective rather than uniformly minimal

The corpus repeatedly alternates compressed research and operations with sparse insight and campaign pages. The governing principle is **density follows the task and reading context**.

For a live presentation, show the evidence needed to carry the spoken argument. For a read-ahead, allow more explanatory detail while preserving entry point, groups and clear takeaway. Both use the same design grammar. Do not call a dense page successful merely because it fits.

Use these density decisions:

- A reveal, insight or statement needs one principal thought and only essential context.
- A proof page can hold a chart or a few comparable evidence units and their interpretation.
- A product or campaign page needs a dominant embodiment and concise explanation of what it promises or makes the audience do.
- A comparison requires enough criteria for a fair decision, with stable rows/columns and a visible conclusion.
- A plan can carry phases, activities and accountability; move exhaustive line items or detailed assumptions to follow-up pages or appendix.

When content overflows, repair in this order: remove duplication; shorten explanation while preserving meaning; move supporting detail to notes or appendix; split at a genuine conceptual boundary; then adjust spacing modestly. Do not shrink text below readability simply to maintain slide count. Do not split evidence from its key qualification.

The corpus's tiny paragraphs are a caution, not a density target. Likewise, a page with one vague sentence is not automatically premium. Minimalism needs a precise claim; density needs navigable structure.

## 6. Imagery: meaning, selection, crop and credibility

Choose imagery according to its role:

- Human context: shows a relevant behavior, desire, barrier, situation or feeling.
- Product evidence: makes the object, use, feature or benefit understandable.
- Creative expression: embodies the campaign's promise or metaphor.
- Execution evidence: makes a proposed asset, channel placement or user action concrete.
- Documentary proof: supplies real context or substantiates a claim.

Do not add a stock image because a template expects one. Prefer a specific, believable scene over a generic smiling team. Human photos should relate to the actual audience; avoid unsupported stereotypes. If purchaser and user differ, attach the image and evidence to the right role.

### Crop with intention

Identify the subject and relevant action before choosing the frame. Preserve the meaningful gesture, product interface or facial expression. Remove irrelevant background. Keep space in the direction of gaze or action when it supports the reading path. Avoid accidental amputations, cropped product names needed for comprehension, and tiny full photographs that surrender their emotional content.

Use a large scene when context is the proof; a close crop when expression or product detail is the proof; a cutout when the object needs a clean relationship with labels or adjacent evidence. A cutout needs clean edges, plausible scale and an intentional anchor. Avoid random floating products, arbitrary rotation and mismatched lighting in composite scenes.

For full-bleed or broad imagery, put text in an uncluttered region or create sufficient tonal separation. An overlay may protect readability, but it should not muddy the image. If the text requires an opaque panel, reconsider whether the image is still contributing. Never place critical copy over faces, detailed objects or unpredictable highlights.

### Scale images to importance

One strong image often communicates more than a collage of generic thumbnails. A storyboard or evidence montage is appropriate when sequence or range is the claim. Keep frame proportions, labels and chronology consistent. A montage should have a clear leading image or a genuinely equal comparison; avoid random overlapping rectangles.

### Show execution as experience

An OOH image should convey the creative and viewing context. A retail visual should show shelf, display or shopper action. A phone screen should show the relevant state and interaction at readable scale. If screen details matter, use a focused crop or short sequence; if the page only needs to establish a touchpoint, a contextual mockup may suffice.

Pair artifacts with a brief account of audience trigger -> action -> response -> intended outcome. Do not substitute a phone shell, creator headshot or platform logo for this mechanism. Mark illustrative mockups and proposed partnerships honestly. Avoid unsupported implication that an asset is produced, approved or deployed.

## 7. Shapes, emphasis and restraint

Every shape should have a semantic or compositional job: group related material, frame evidence, mark a stage, express a relation, carry a takeaway, or establish contrast for a focal object. Decorative shapes are allowed when part of a current campaign idea, but they must not become independent focal points.

Use borders sparingly. Repeated boxes often announce uncertainty about grouping. Try whitespace, alignment and a short subhead first. Rounded cards are appropriate for distinct modules or interface-like content; they are not a default for all slides. Keep corner radii, rule weights and internal padding coherent.

Arrows mean direction, dependency, sequence or transformation. Lines mean connection; arrowheads mean something stronger. Do not connect every object to every other object. A diagram with many unlabeled arrows is rarely explanatory.

Use icons only when their meaning is immediate and useful. Keep one icon family and consistent stroke/scale. If removing an icon improves comprehension, remove it. Do not use pictograms as data encodings unless quantity, denominator and proportionality are clear.

An emphasis field, underline, accent word or large numeral should point to the argument's key distinction. Apply emphasis consistently across comparable content. Avoid simultaneously using glow, shadow, gradient, outline, oversized text, icon and badge to mark the same claim.

Shadows may establish believable physicality in a product mockup; they should not make every paragraph look like a UI tile. Textures and motifs can support a concept or culture, but their contrast should recede behind the reading layer.

## 8. Color as a functional system

Choose colors from the new brand or brief, never from a reference company's palette. Define functional roles: ground, primary text, secondary text, principal emphasis, optional secondary emphasis, comparison series and semantic status. A small set of roles is more useful than a large palette.

Use neutral or quiet grounds for detailed analysis, and strong fields when a concept, section or campaign moment merits them. Light and dark backgrounds are contrast decisions inside one language. Preserve type roles, spacing and meaning when switching ground.

Concentrate saturation around the selected fact, phase or idea. Unselected evidence should remain readable but quieter. Do not rely on color alone for a distinction: combine it with labels, ordering, weight or shape. Avoid accidental meanings such as red indicating both an important brand fact and negative performance on the same page.

Keep chart colors stable across pages for the same entities. Preserve semantic status meaning. Use additional categorical colors only when the comparison requires them, with direct labels and sufficient distinction. Do not apply a rainbow because there are many items.

Test actual contrast in the rendered result. Pale accent text on white, fine gold on dark scenes, and body copy on transparent photo panels are recurring weak points in the source; do not reproduce them. Evaluate image overlays and screenshot labels separately from editable text.

## 9. Data: proof that leads to a decision

Begin with the conclusion to be supported, then choose an encoding that allows an honest comparison. A chart is successful when the audience can see the relevant difference or trend and understand its implication without reconstructing an external spreadsheet.

Use bars for magnitude or category comparison, lines for change over ordered time, dots for position or paired comparisons, and simple stacked bars for meaningful composition. A few directly labeled parts may justify a donut; many similar wedges or multiple 3D pies obscure differences. These are transferable encoding recommendations, not a claim that the corpus always follows them.

Keep honest scales. Bars normally start at zero; if a line-chart range is narrowed to reveal variation, make the scale clear. Do not manufacture a funnel's taper, bubble area, progress gauge or pictogram count unless it corresponds to the data. A conceptual funnel may show stages, but it must not pretend to be quantified conversion.

Use direct labels where feasible. Give title, units, period, geography or population, and source enough visibility to prevent misreading. Retain sample size and methodological limitations when material. Distinguish percentage from percentage-point change. Identify whether a metric is actual, baseline, target, estimate or scenario. Do not present historical reference figures as current evidence.

Select the critical series, point or gap with an accent; mute the rest modestly. Keep comparable axes consistent in small multiples. Annotate the event, gap or change that matters. An interpretation near the evidence can be a bottom conclusion or adjacent sentence, but it should say what follows rather than repeat the number.

Separate data from assumption and hypothesis. A forecast should reveal its drivers and scenario, not only a large promised total. A metric tile should show context such as period or baseline. A broad claim cannot be supported by a convenient number from a different population.

### Tables and comparison

Use stable criteria and parallel wording. Align numeric columns, preserve units, and avoid unnecessary grid lines. Group related rows; emphasize the decisive row, column or total without hiding counterevidence. The decision should be visible, but the comparison must remain fair.

A competitor map requires meaningful axes and defensible positions. A matrix is useful when intersections matter. SWOT, 3C and similar frameworks are analytic aids: select consequential findings and synthesize them. Filling a framework with equal amounts of text is not a strategy.

## 10. Communication roles within the single language

These roles describe the page's job. They are not separate visual styles or prescribed layouts. Choose relationship, hierarchy and density from the contract in section 2.

| Page job | Primary judgment | What earns space | Typical repair |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cover | Establish topic, promise and relevance immediately | Clear name/promise, one strong contextual or product visual, quiet metadata | Remove agenda, excessive credits or competing badges |
| Executive summary | Preserve the argument and requested decision | Problem/opportunity, audience, insight, recommendation, delivery and outcome at a useful level | Replace miniature reproductions of all pages with a concise decision story |
| Section transition | Reset attention and orient the next question | Short section cue or forward-looking claim; deliberate breathing room | Shorten content, keep navigation subordinate |
| Evidence/content | Prove one material conclusion | Relevant chart, comparison or grouped research plus implication | Remove unrelated facts; make headline state meaning |
| Statement | Make one consequential thought unmistakable | Precise claim; essential qualifier or visual context | Sharpen vague copy instead of decorating it |
| Insight | Reveal a behavior-producing tension | Observation, desire/barrier, concise interpretation; human scene when useful | Distinguish observation from inferred explanation; avoid generic aspirational language |
| Audience | Explain whom to change and why | Relevant behavior, motivation, barrier, context and evidence; purchaser/user distinction | Remove incidental demographic trivia and invented quotations |
| Brand role/positioning | Make the brand's right to help credible | Audience need, relevant capability, promise and reason to believe | Replace broad virtue claims with an explicit mechanism |
| Product | Make the benefit visible and believable | Dominant product/use image, few relevant features, benefit and proof | Reduce floating callouts; connect feature to audience value |
| Campaign idea | Make the creative expression memorable | Short campaign phrase, key visual, link to insight and brand promise | Remove execution clutter from the reveal; add missing rationale on an adjacent proof page |
| Execution | Show how the idea works in the world | Artifact or sequence, context, audience action, intended response | Replace generic platform logos with a tangible mechanic |
| Comparison | Support a fair choice | Common criteria, aligned evidence, decisive difference and qualification | Normalize unequal presentation and expose assumptions |
| Journey/plan | Make sequence and accountability understandable | Stage objective, audience action, asset/channel role, timing and measure | Separate content, channel, calendar and financial detail when one matrix becomes unreadable |
| Measurement | Explain how success will be recognized | Baseline/target, definition, period, measurement method and relevant cost | Distinguish outputs from outcomes and estimated from actual results |
| Closing | Consolidate value and make the next action clear | Brief final recommendation, decision request or next step; a restrained visual echo | Replace an empty thank-you when the audience needs a decision |
| Appendix | Preserve rigor without interrupting the narrative | Assumptions, sources, methods, detailed tables and operating plans | Add descriptive navigation; maintain readable hierarchy despite higher density |

### Insight and strategy deserve special care

An observation reports something seen. An insight explains why it matters to behavior. A strategic approach specifies the change and the brand's way of enabling it. A campaign idea expresses that approach memorably. Do not rename the same sentence four times.

A useful internal reasoning chain is: the audience wants X, but Y prevents or complicates it; this produces behavior Z; the brand can credibly help through capability A; therefore the campaign invites action B. Write it in human terms. Show only the parts needed on each page. Do not force truth/tension/motivation or consumer/category/company diagrams into every deck.

### Campaign and operating detail

Separate a creative reveal from its full operating plan when detail would dilute the reveal. Then make the plan accountable: who encounters what, where, in what order, why they would participate, what friction is removed, and what response is intended. Creator selection needs audience fit and a role, not just fame. A partnership needs a mechanism. An event needs a participant experience. A content pillar needs a purpose. A channel needs a distribution job. A metric needs a definition.

## 11. Rhythm across a deck

A deck should feel like a sequence of decisions, not a slideshow of unrelated templates. Preserve visual anchors while changing the energy required by the argument.

Evidence builds credibility; synthesis converts it into direction; human or product imagery makes the direction felt; a concept reveal supplies memorability; concrete artifacts make it feasible; a plan and measures make it accountable. Alternate these functions as the narrative requires. Do not enforce a fixed numerical ratio or one decorative change every few slides.

After several dense evidence pages, a concise synthesis or insight often helps absorption. After a bold idea page, follow with rationale or tangible proof. A section transition should mark a real change in audience question, not interrupt arbitrarily. A timeline and budget usually belong after the audience understands the idea they fund.

Read all headlines in sequence. They should carry the story's logic without body text. If they form only a list of topics, improve the narrative. Check transitions: what question does the previous slide create, and does the next one answer it?

Thumbnail the deck. Look for prolonged visual sameness, constant full saturation, repeated equally dense grids, and disconnected image treatment. Repair through changes in content emphasis, relationship, image scale and breathing room while keeping the same underlying type/grid/role system. Do not introduce a new palette or typeface merely to avoid repetition.

Maintain a visual echo between opening and closing through a related motif, subject or promise, without repeating the cover mechanically. Final pages should preserve the appropriate level of energy and confidence rather than devolve into unstyled administrative content.

## 12. Calibrated implementation guidance for PPT Master

The following values are **starting recommendations**, not measurements extracted from raster reference pages. Judge them in the final rendered slide and actual delivery mode. They may vary with language, font metrics, content and client requirements.

For a typical 16:9 deck, begin with roughly 4-6% horizontal safe margins, a comparable intentional top/bottom safe area, and a reusable spacing unit. Use a small column grid with clearly defined gutters. Keep title/evidence/source positions stable enough to be learned by the reader. Permit intentional full-bleed imagery and oversized campaign words without breaking text safety.

For a 13.333 x 7.5-inch canvas, a useful initial hierarchy is approximately 30-40 pt for analytical headlines, 20-24 pt for supporting subheads, 18-24 pt for live-presentation body text, and 12-14 pt for readable captions/sources. Short statement/campaign text may range around 44-72 pt or larger when the phrase and composition justify it. Read-ahead detail can be smaller after explicit readability checks; it should not silently become projected microtext. Use font metrics and render tests to refine these ranges.

Aim for one dominant focal object and two or three supporting groups on ordinary proof pages. This is a diagnostic, not a rigid content limit. A comparison or operating table can contain more units if grouping and reading context support them. When numerous groups need independent explanation, use additional pages.

Create semantic tokens rather than exact layouts:

- Typography roles and line-height behavior.
- Safe margins, column/gutter system and spacing scale.
- Light/dark ground and associated text contrast.
- Accent, comparison and status meanings.
- Metric/label/unit treatment.
- Image frame, crop, caption and cutout behavior.
- Table headers, rules, numeric alignment and selected-row treatment.
- Diagram connectors, stages and emphasis treatment.
- Source/navigation treatment.

All page roles should use these same tokens. PPT Master may implement reusable composition operations—span, align, compare, sequence, highlight, crop, annotate and synthesize—without creating separate style packs.

Keep text, charts, tables and diagrams editable when possible. Preserve meaningful reading order and accessible text contrast. Check language glyphs and font availability. If the implementation substitutes a font, inspect line wrapping and relative hierarchy again. Do not rasterize entire authored slides just to force visual consistency.

This specification is ready to guide implementation, but no existing PPT Master configuration or master file was supplied. Do not imply automatic integration. An authoring agent should map these semantic roles into the actual tool's supported format when that system is available.

## 13. Review and repair: judgment that survives the source

Review first at thumbnail size, then at presentation size, then at close reading. Each scale exposes different defects.

### Thumbnail review

Can the eye identify one entry point? Is the page a deliberate composition or a collection of evenly weighted objects? Is the claimed relationship visible? Is the deck's density rhythm purposeful? Does emphasis mean the same thing from page to page?

If the eye cannot choose, remove competing emphasis, increase scale separation or simplify groups. If the page looks sparse but says little, sharpen its claim. If it resembles a generic dashboard, replace interchangeable cards with the actual relationship among facts.

### Presentation-size review

Can the intended audience read essential copy and labels? Does the headline match the evidence? Is the image crop understandable? Do diagrams reveal the mechanism? Can the audience explain what matters without reading every sentence?

If text is cramped, edit and decompose. If an image feels incidental, replace or remove it; enlarging irrelevant photography is not a solution. If a chart is technically readable but directionless, state its implication and highlight the meaningful evidence.

### Close-reading review

Check data, source scope, units, dates, sample size, assumptions, actual/target distinctions, calculation consistency, alignment, diacritics, line breaks, captions, crop edges and clipping. Confirm that proposals and mockups are labeled appropriately. Inspect each rendered slide, not just the source file.

### Common symptoms and concrete repairs

| Symptom | Likely cause | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| Every slide looks like a title and three cards | Layout chosen before argument | Recompose as the actual comparison, sequence, tension or proof |
| Beautiful but strategically empty | Imagery or slogan lacks a causal brand role | Add audience tension, credible capability and intended behavior; edit visuals around them |
| Dense page has no entry point | Equal-weight headings, boxes and highlights | Select one takeaway; group evidence; move secondary explanation |
| Sparse page feels generic | Vague claim and irrelevant stock image | Write a specific supported conclusion and select a meaningful visual |
| Numbers look impressive but say little | No baseline, denominator, period or implication | Add context and interpretation; correct uncertainty |
| Campaign feels like a moodboard | Too many assets without audience action | Select the key expression, then show concrete execution mechanics |
| Product feels pasted onto the slide | Weak scale, edge quality, grounding or benefit relationship | Improve cutout/crop, establish anchor and connect to the relevant promise |
| Photo page is hard to read | Text overlays a complex or low-contrast region | Recrop, relocate text or create controlled contrast |
| Flow diagram feels complicated | Decorative connectors or multiple independent stories | Label relationships, remove unnecessary arrows, split independent paths |
| Comparison feels biased | Unequal criteria, scale or verbal detail | Normalize treatment and include relevant counterevidence |
| Deck has no rhythm | Constant density and focal structure | Insert genuine synthesis and embodiment where the narrative needs them |
| Deck feels inconsistent | Unstable type, margins, semantic color or image treatment | Restore shared tokens; retain purposeful composition variation |

### Acceptance gate

Do not approve a deck while any essential evidence is unreadable, a claim is unsupported, data is misleading, text is clipped, hierarchy is ambiguous, or a required decision is unclear. Craft cannot override correctness.

Assess every page for: argument clarity, first-glance hierarchy, relationship clarity, legibility, alignment/spacing, image relevance/crop, evidence integrity, restraint, and connection to surrounding pages. Describe the defect and repair concretely; avoid vague feedback such as “make it premium.”

## 14. Reference-free authoring protocol

### Resolve apparent conflicts through the higher principle

If a reference is sparse and another is crowded, preserve task-appropriate density, not a compromise amount of text. If one is symmetric and another asymmetric, preserve the readable relationship and balanced visual weight. If one uses a photograph and another a clean diagram, preserve meaningful evidence, not a compulsory media ratio. If one uses a topic heading and another a full-sentence headline, preserve clear purpose and narrative continuity. If one is restrained and another expressive, preserve selective emphasis: creative intensity is earned by the idea's role in the story.

When requirements compete, prioritize factual integrity and readability, then argument/hierarchy, then relational clarity and rhythm, then polish and decorative expression. A client's valid brand constraints inform every layer, but they do not justify unreadable essential information. Improve contrast or composition within those constraints.

### Four original transfer exercises

These are invented teaching examples, not copied reference layouts, factual recommendations or mini presentations.

**Research to implication.** Suppose a supplied survey shows high awareness but low repeat purchase, with convenience named as a barrier. Do not give awareness, repeat purchase and convenience three decorative cards with equal prominence. State the supported purchasing or retention issue, compare only matched bases and periods, show repeat-purchase evidence as a separately scoped observation when its base differs, attach the barrier evidence, and end with a bounded implication. Keep sample and scope visible. The visual's job is to explain the issue, not celebrate all three metrics or fabricate a tracked funnel.

**Audience tension to brand role.** Suppose interviews indicate people want an easier morning routine but resist adding another step. A generic happy-family image and a slogan about happiness do not preserve the insight. Make the desire/barrier relation clear, use a relevant routine scene if available, and specify how the product removes rather than adds friction. Keep proposed effect separate from demonstrated capability. The emotional image supports the argument rather than replacing it.

**Concept to execution.** Suppose a campaign invites users to share a small everyday achievement. Reveal the invitation clearly, then show a plausible submission path and how participants receive value. Explain the creator/content/channel role and measurement on the relevant follow-up pages. Do not populate a collage with every social platform. Specific mechanics make the creative credible.

**Complex plan to readable commitment.** Suppose a plan has four stages, ten channels and twenty cost lines. First expose the strategic sequence, stage outcomes and major investment choices. Use additional operational detail or appendix for channel calendar and line-item costs. Keep totals and assumptions traceable. The decision page needs enough information for commitment; the operating record needs enough detail for delivery. Both retain the same typography, alignment and emphasis grammar.

A future agent that has never seen the corpus should follow this sequence:

1. Read this entire specification; obtain the brief, audience, delivery context, decision, factual evidence and authorized brand/assets.
2. Draft the narrative and identify gaps in evidence. Preserve uncertainty instead of inventing support.
3. Write a contract for each planned page. Edit weak takeaways before selecting visual relationships.
4. Establish the single semantic token system from the current brief. Do not borrow reference palettes or create style categories.
5. Compose each page around its focal object and argument. Choose density, image scale and relationships deliberately.
6. Make product and campaign promises tangible through credible use or execution artifacts.
7. Link objectives, activities, cost and measurement, at the level required for the decision.
8. Review headline sequence and thumbnail rhythm. Then render and inspect every page at useful reading size.
9. Repair hierarchy, evidence, spacing, crops, typography and correctness; repeat inspection of affected pages and deck transitions.
10. Deliver an editable deck with sources/assumptions and a clear final decision or next step, when presentation creation is actually requested.

### Expertise-preservation test

If the reference corpus vanished, this document still supplies the reasoning spine, hierarchy system, spatial grammar, type behavior, density decisions, image judgment, color semantics, data integrity rules, page-role logic, rhythm, implementation starting points and repair criteria. It does not depend on seeing or copying a reference slide.

Test transfer by asking an authoring agent to explain its decisions for an unfamiliar brief: why this focal object, why this amount of evidence, why this crop, why this relationship, why this emphasis, why this next slide? Good explanations should connect these choices to the audience and supported argument. “Because the template uses it” is not enough.

The final standard is not resemblance to a remembered page. It is consistent professional judgment: the right evidence, in the right relationship, at the right scale, with enough restraint to make the marketing argument clear and memorable.
